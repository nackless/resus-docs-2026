Often times, we are told by someone that a new research is out and we then start doing things that are the recommendations and change oir practice.

How often do we ask the important questions that would probably decide if this should actually happen. 

As a matter of fact, is 1 study enough to change practice?

These are some of the questions worth asking.
1. How many patients of what age, gender and disease severity were a part of study?
2. Did any patients what's from the study, if so how many and why?
3. What was the criteria for saying someone has been cured?
4. Were there any set inclusion or exclusion criteria and did they change the demographic significantly to make the study results more favourable?
5. Have there been more studies similar to this and are the results of the study significantly different from previous results?
6. Was it a randomized controlled trial? 
7. What were the statistical tests used to prove the authors point and were they correctly chosen? 

If you are a clinician who use evidence based research then it becomes important for you to consider these questions before you start advocating for a new method or approach.
