Often times, we are told by someone that a new research is out and we then start doing things that are the recommendations and change our practice.

How often do we ask the important questions that would decide if this change should actually happen?

<ObsidianCallout type="warning" title="EVIDENCE PRACTICE PEARL">
  Is 1 single study enough to change clinical practice? Always evaluate trial demographics, drop-out rates, and statistical validity before modifying emergency department protocols.
</ObsidianCallout>

These are some of the critical questions worth asking:

1. **Patient Cohort:** How many patients of what age, gender, and disease severity were a part of the study?
2. **Withdrawals:** Did any patients withdraw from the study? If so, how many and why?
3. **Cure Criteria:** What was the exact clinical criteria for stating someone has been cured?
4. **Demographics:** Were there set inclusion or exclusion criteria, and did they change the demographics to make results more favorable?
5. **Replication:** Have there been other studies similar to this, and are the results significantly different from previous trials?
6. **Study Design:** Was it a randomized controlled trial (RCT)?
7. **Biostatistics:** What statistical tests were used to prove the authors' point, and were they correctly chosen?

If you are a clinician who uses evidence-based research, it becomes essential to consider these questions before advocating for a new clinical approach.
